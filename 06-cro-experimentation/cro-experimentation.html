Conversion Rate Optimization & Experimentation

Identifying conversion friction, designing structured experiments, and evaluating whether landing-page improvements can translate into stronger ecommerce performance.

Planning & Testing Framework

Business Context

This CRO analysis was designed for the Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream ecommerce case study. The objective is to identify opportunities to reduce conversion friction and improve the percentage of visitors who complete a purchase.

Product Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream
Primary Conversion Completed Purchase

Initial Conversion Problem

The starting scenario assumes that the landing page receives 1,000 visitors and generates 50 purchases.

Initial conversion rate:
50 purchases ÷ 1,000 visitors × 100 = 5%

Potential Sources of Conversion Friction

  • Unclear product value proposition
  • Insufficient price visibility
  • Lack of perceived product relevance
  • Insufficient offer or savings information

Experiment Design

A structured A/B test was used to compare the existing landing-page experience against an improved version.

Version A Original experience
Version B Improved experience

Experiment Objective

Determine whether the improved landing-page experience produces a higher observed purchase conversion rate than the original version.

Key Measurement

Purchase conversion rate is the primary success metric because the business objective is to increase completed purchases rather than optimize engagement in isolation.

Test Results

The observed test results show a higher purchase conversion rate for Version B.

1,000 Visitors · Version A
1,000 Visitors · Version B
5% → 6.5% Conversion Rate
Metric Version A Version B
Visitors 1,000 1,000
Purchases 50 65
Conversion Rate 5% 6.5%

Experiment Analysis

Absolute Improvement

6.5% − 5% = 1.5 percentage points

Relative Improvement

(6.5% − 5%) ÷ 5% × 100 = 30%

Version B produced a 1.5 percentage-point increase in observed conversion rate, representing a 30% relative improvement over Version A.

Initial Decision

Version B is the initial winner based on the observed purchase conversion rate.

However, the result should not automatically be treated as conclusive. A responsible CRO decision requires additional validation before permanently rolling out the variant.

Why Further Validation Matters

  • Confirm that the sample size is sufficient.
  • Evaluate statistical significance.
  • Confirm traffic was appropriately allocated.
  • Check for external factors that could influence results.
  • Verify that the improvement persists over time.

Next Steps

  1. Validate the experiment with sufficient sample size.
  2. Confirm that traffic is evenly and appropriately allocated between variants.
  3. Monitor add-to-cart and checkout initiation rates.
  4. Evaluate whether conversion improvement translates into improved CAC and contribution margin.
  5. Continue testing other potential sources of conversion friction.
Optimization principle: CRO should not stop after finding a winning variant. The goal is to build a repeatable experimentation process that connects conversion improvements to broader business outcomes.

What I Would Analyze Next

  • Landing-page engagement and drop-off points
  • Add-to-cart and checkout initiation rates
  • Conversion rate by traffic source
  • Conversion rate by device
  • Customer acquisition cost before and after the change
  • Contribution margin impact
  • Repeat purchase behavior
  • Additional landing-page experiments
Case Study Status

Current Status & Next Phase

The CRO framework and initial A/B test analysis have been defined. The observed test results indicate that Version B outperformed Version A on purchase conversion rate.

The next analytical phase would be to validate the result with sufficient data and determine whether the conversion improvement produces a meaningful improvement in acquisition efficiency and profitability.
