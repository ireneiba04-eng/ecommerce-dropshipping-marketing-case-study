# CRO & Experimentation

## Product

Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream

## Objective

The objective of the CRO analysis is to identify landing-page improvements that may increase purchase conversion rate while maintaining commercially sustainable customer acquisition economics.

The analysis focuses on reducing conversion friction and improving the clarity of the product's value proposition.

## Initial Conversion Problem

Assume the landing page receives 1,000 visitors and generates 50 purchases.

Purchase conversion rate:

50 / 1,000 × 100 = 5%

Potential sources of conversion friction include:

- Unclear product value proposition

- Price visibility

- Lack of perceived product relevance

- Insufficient offer or savings information

- Slow page loading

- Confusing or cluttered page design

- Weak call-to-action

## CRO Hypothesis

If the landing page clearly communicates Hoigi's primary benefit, target problem, price, and call-to-action above the fold, purchase conversion rate may increase because visitors can understand the product's value with less effort.

## A/B Test

### Version A — Control

The existing landing-page presentation.

### Version B — Test

A clearer hero section containing:

- Dry, itchy skin?

- Everyday hydration at an affordable price.

- Product image

- Clearly displayed price

- Primary call-to-action

- Brief explanation of the product's main benefit

The product price itself is not changed in this experiment so that the test primarily evaluates value-proposition clarity rather than price elasticity.

## Test Results

| Metric | Version A | Version B |

|---|---:|---:|

| Visitors | 1,000 | 1,000 |

| Purchases | 50 | 65 |

| Conversion Rate | 5% | 6.5% |

Version B produced a higher observed purchase conversion rate.

Absolute improvement:

6.5% - 5% = 1.5 percentage points

Relative improvement:

(6.5% - 5%) / 5% × 100 = 30%

## Initial Decision

Version B is the initial winner based on the observed conversion rate.

However, the result should not automatically be treated as definitive. Further validation should consider sample size, statistical significance, traffic quality, audience comparability, test duration, and other factors that could influence the result.

## Next Steps

1. Validate the experiment with sufficient sample size.

2. Confirm that traffic is evenly and appropriately allocated between variants.

3. Monitor add-to-cart and checkout initiation rates.

4. Evaluate whether the conversion improvement translates into improved CAC and contribution margin.

5. Continue testing other potential sources of conversion friction.
