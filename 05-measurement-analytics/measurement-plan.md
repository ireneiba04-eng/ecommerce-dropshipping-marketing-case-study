# Measurement & Analytics Plan

## Product

Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream

## Objective

The objective of the measurement plan is to establish a framework for evaluating marketing performance, customer acquisition efficiency, profitability, and scalability.

The analysis should connect marketing metrics to business outcomes rather than evaluating individual metrics in isolation.

## Primary Conversion

The primary conversion is a completed purchase of the Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream.

The purchase event should be tracked consistently across the customer journey so that advertising platforms and analytics systems can measure conversion performance.

## Key Performance Indicators

### Impressions

Impressions measure the number of times an advertisement is displayed.

Impressions are primarily used to evaluate reach and exposure.

They do not indicate that a customer engaged with or purchased the product.

### Click-Through Rate (CTR)

CTR measures the percentage of impressions that resulted in a click.

Formula:

CTR = Clicks / Impressions × 100

CTR can help evaluate how effectively an advertisement attracts attention and generates interest.

### Cost Per Click (CPC)

CPC measures the average amount spent for each click.

Formula:

CPC = Advertising Spend / Clicks

CPC helps evaluate the cost of generating traffic.

### Conversion Rate

Conversion rate measures the percentage of users who complete a desired conversion after reaching the relevant stage of the funnel.

For website conversion rate:

Conversion Rate = Purchases / Website Visitors × 100

Conversion rate helps evaluate how effectively traffic is converted into customers.

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

CAC measures the average amount spent to acquire a customer.

Formula:

CAC = Acquisition Spend / New Customers Acquired

For example:

$500 advertising spend / 50 customers = $10 CAC

CAC should be evaluated alongside contribution margin rather than treated as an isolated performance metric.

### Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

ROAS measures revenue generated relative to advertising spend.

Formula:

ROAS = Revenue Attributed to Advertising / Advertising Spend

For example, if $500 in advertising generates $1,500 in attributed revenue:

ROAS = $1,500 / $500 = 3.0

This means the campaign generated $3 in attributed revenue for every $1 spent on advertising.

ROAS does not equal profit because it does not account for product costs, payment processing fees, refunds, operating expenses, or other costs.

## Contribution Margin

Contribution margin evaluates how much revenue remains after variable costs required to generate and fulfill the sale.

Relevant variable costs may include:

- Product fulfillment cost

- Payment processing fees

- Shipping costs where applicable

- Refund and return costs

- Advertising costs

- Other variable transaction costs

Contribution margin should be evaluated before determining whether customer acquisition is economically sustainable.

## Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

Customer lifetime value estimates the total value generated by a customer over the expected customer relationship.

LTV becomes particularly important if customers make repeat purchases.

A customer with a higher acquisition cost may still be economically valuable if the customer generates substantial future contribution.

However, LTV should be based on observed customer behavior when sufficient data becomes available rather than relying only on assumptions.

## Attribution

Attribution determines how conversion credit is assigned across marketing touchpoints.

A customer may interact with multiple channels before purchasing.

For example:

TikTok → Website → Meta Retargeting → Purchase

The final conversion may be attributed to Meta under a last-touch model even though TikTok contributed to the customer's initial discovery.

The analysis should therefore consider:

- First-touch attribution

- Last-touch attribution

- Multi-touch attribution where appropriate

- Assisted conversions

- Cross-channel conversion paths

No single attribution model should automatically be treated as the absolute truth.

## Incrementality

Incrementality evaluates whether marketing activity caused additional conversions that would not have occurred without the marketing intervention.

This is important because an attributed conversion is not necessarily an incremental conversion.

For example, a customer may already have been highly likely to purchase before receiving a retargeting advertisement.

Where practical, experiments or controlled comparisons should be used to estimate incremental impact.

## Marginal CAC

Marginal CAC measures the acquisition cost associated with additional customers generated by additional advertising spend.

Formula:

Marginal CAC = Additional Advertising Spend / Additional Customers Acquired

Marginal CAC is important when scaling because the average CAC may remain attractive while the cost of acquiring additional customers increases.

## Marginal ROAS

Marginal ROAS evaluates the revenue generated by additional advertising spend.

Formula:

Marginal ROAS = Additional Revenue / Additional Advertising Spend

Marginal performance should be monitored when increasing budgets because historical average performance does not guarantee that additional spending will perform at the same efficiency.

## Measurement Framework

The campaign should be evaluated across the full funnel:

Impressions

→ Clicks

→ Website Visits

→ Add to Cart

→ Checkout

→ Purchase

→ Repeat Purchase

Each stage should be measured to identify where performance improves or deteriorates.

## Decision Rules

The following framework will be used when evaluating campaign performance:

- Low CTR may indicate that creative, messaging, or audience targeting needs improvement.

- High CPC may indicate inefficient traffic acquisition.

- High traffic but low conversion rate may indicate problems with the landing page, offer, pricing, or product-market fit.

- Low CAC with weak contribution margin may not represent sustainable profitability.

- Strong attributed ROAS does not automatically prove incremental profitability.

- Increasing marginal CAC may indicate that a channel is approaching saturation.

- Strong LTV relative to CAC may support continued acquisition investment.

- Performance should be evaluated across multiple metrics rather than using one KPI in isolation.

## Measurement Priorities

The initial measurement priorities are:

1. Track purchases accurately.

2. Measure CTR and CPC by channel.

3. Measure website conversion rate.

4. Calculate CAC by channel.

5. Calculate attributed ROAS.

6. Evaluate contribution margin after acquisition costs.

7. Analyze customer journeys and attribution.

8. Evaluate incrementality where practical.

9. Monitor marginal CAC and marginal ROAS during budget increases.

10. Evaluate LTV as sufficient customer data becomes available.

## Current Status

The measurement framework has been defined as an initial analytical plan.

Actual campaign data is not yet available.

Future decisions should be based on observed performance rather than hypothetical assumptions.
