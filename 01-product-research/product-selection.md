# Product Selection — Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream

## Product Overview

The selected product for this e-commerce case study is the **Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream**, identified through product research on CJ Dropshipping.

The product was selected as a candidate for further evaluation based on its positioning within the skincare and beauty category, its potential customer demand, and the initial supplier and fulfillment information available through CJ Dropshipping.

## Supplier

**Supplier Platform:** CJ Dropshipping

CJ Dropshipping was selected as the supplier platform for this project after comparing available sourcing and fulfillment options.

The purpose of the supplier evaluation is to determine whether the product can be sourced and fulfilled at a cost that allows for sustainable customer acquisition and contribution margin.

## Initial Product Information

Based on the product listing reviewed during the initial research:

- Product: Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream

- Product category: Skincare / Beauty

- Product size: 50 ml

- Supplier platform: CJ Dropshipping

- Initial shipping information: Shipping was displayed as $0 for the option reviewed

- Estimated processing time: 1–3 days for the stated majority of orders

- Estimated delivery time: 3–5 days for the shipping option reviewed

These figures represent the information available during the initial product evaluation and should be re-verified before launch.

## Why This Product Was Selected

The product was selected for further research because it appeared to have potential within the skincare market and provided an opportunity to evaluate:

- Product-market fit

- Competitive positioning

- Supplier economics

- Fulfillment feasibility

- Customer acquisition economics

- Conversion potential

- Contribution margin

- Scalability

The product is therefore being treated as a **testable business hypothesis**, rather than assuming that the product will be successful.

## Customer Problem

The initial marketing hypothesis is that potential customers may be interested in skincare products focused on moisturizing, skin appearance, and overall skin care.

Before launch, customer research will be used to validate:

- Customer needs

- Pain points

- Purchase motivations

- Objections

- Price sensitivity

- Existing alternatives

- Desired product benefits

## Product Positioning

The initial positioning hypothesis will focus on the product's skincare and moisturizing value proposition.

Any product benefits or performance claims presented in future marketing materials will need to be evaluated against the supplier's available documentation and applicable advertising requirements.

The project will avoid treating supplier marketing claims as independently verified evidence.

## Competitive Considerations

Competitive research will be used to evaluate:

- Competitor pricing

- Product size

- Product features

- Ingredients and formulation claims

- Brand positioning

- Customer reviews

- Shipping expectations

- Promotional strategies

- Perceived value

Established skincare brands will be used as benchmarks to determine whether the selected product can offer a compelling value proposition.

## Initial Business Hypothesis

The initial hypothesis is:

> If the product can be sourced and fulfilled at a competitive cost, positioned around a clearly defined customer need, and marketed to an appropriate audience, then it may be possible to acquire customers at a sustainable cost.

This hypothesis will be tested rather than assumed to be true.

## Unit Economics — To Be Validated

Before allocating a meaningful advertising budget, the following costs must be established:

- Product cost

- Shipping cost

- Payment processing fees

- Fulfillment-related costs

- Applicable taxes or duties

- Advertising cost

- Other variable costs

The initial unit-economics model will calculate:

**Revenue − Variable Costs = Contribution Margin**

The project will then evaluate whether the contribution margin can support customer acquisition.

Key metrics to be evaluated include:

- CPA — Cost Per Acquisition

- CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost

- AOV — Average Order Value

- ROAS — Return on Ad Spend

- Contribution Margin

- Contribution After CAC

## Key Risks

Several factors require validation before launch:

1. Product demand may be insufficient.

2. Competitive pricing may limit margin.

3. Actual shipping costs may differ from the initial listing.

4. Delivery times may vary by destination.

5. Advertising costs may make customer acquisition uneconomical.

6. Product claims may require additional substantiation.

7. Conversion rate may be lower than expected.

8. Customer acquisition may not be scalable.

## Go / No-Go Criteria

The product should not be scaled solely because it generates clicks or sales.

A positive recommendation will require evidence that:

- Customers are responding to the offer.

- Conversion performance is acceptable.

- Variable costs are understood.

- Contribution margin is sufficient.

- CAC is economically sustainable.

- Customer acquisition can be scaled without unacceptable deterioration in efficiency.
