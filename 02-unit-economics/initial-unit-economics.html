02 · UNIT ECONOMICS

Unit Economics Analysis

Evaluating product economics, customer acquisition costs, contribution margin, and the conditions required for sustainable growth.

Product

Hoigi Moisturizing Skin Cream

Supplier platform: CJ Dropshipping

This analysis evaluates the initial unit economics of the selected product using a hypothetical selling price and customer acquisition scenario.

Initial Assumptions

  • Selling price: $24.99
  • Product fulfillment cost: $9.99
  • Payment processing fee: 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction
  • Advertising spend: $500
  • New customers acquired: 50

These figures are used for initial analysis and are not yet a complete representation of total business profitability.

Gross Profit

Gross Profit = Selling Price − Product Fulfillment Cost

$24.99 − $9.99 = $15.00

Gross profit per order = $15.00

Gross Margin

Gross Margin = Gross Profit ÷ Selling Price × 100

$15.00 ÷ $24.99 × 100 ≈ 60.02%

Gross margin ≈ 60.02%

Payment Processing Fee

Payment Processing Fee = (Selling Price × 2.9%) + $0.30

($24.99 × 0.029) + $0.30 = $1.02471

Payment processing fee ≈ $1.02 per order

Contribution Before Customer Acquisition Cost

Contribution Before CAC = Gross Profit − Payment Processing Fee

$15.00 − $1.02471 = $13.97529

Contribution before CAC ≈ $13.98 per order

This amount represents the contribution available before customer acquisition costs and other variable or operating expenses.

Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC = Advertising Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired

$500 ÷ 50 = $10.00

CAC = $10.00 per customer

Contribution After CAC

Contribution After CAC = Contribution Before CAC − CAC

$13.98 − $10.00 = $3.98

Contribution after CAC ≈ $3.98 per acquired customer/order

Initial Interpretation

Based on these assumptions, the product generates approximately $3.98 of contribution after customer acquisition cost.

However, this does not represent final net profit.

Additional costs that still need to be evaluated include:

  • Shipping and fulfillment costs not already included in the fulfillment assumption
  • Transaction and platform fees
  • Refunds and returns
  • Customer service
  • Software
  • Store/platform costs
  • Taxes
  • Other operating expenses
  • Additional variable costs
The current analysis should therefore be treated as an initial contribution-margin assessment rather than proof of final profitability.

Scaling Recommendation

The initial results do not justify immediately increasing the advertising budget substantially.

Before scaling, the business should:

  1. Validate the product and fulfillment economics.
  2. Test customer acquisition performance.
  3. Measure CAC and conversion performance.
  4. Evaluate contribution margin.
  5. Analyze attribution across marketing channels.
  6. Evaluate incrementality.
  7. Monitor marginal CAC and marginal ROAS.
  8. Increase the advertising budget incrementally if performance remains sustainable.

A small-budget testing approach is preferable to aggressively scaling before the underlying economics have been validated.

Current Status

Initial unit economics are promising but incomplete.

The product should proceed to further testing and analysis rather than being considered definitively profitable at this stage.
